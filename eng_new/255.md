THE WAYWARD
When the future came, it brought with it a new age of technology. The
digital and the physical bleed together, blurring the line between both.
Nature will not be stopped, though; it finds a way. Wherever you may be
in this chromed age, it finds you—the Wayward.
The most overt injection the playbook pushes into the setting is that there
are other places out there, beyond urban sprawls that are prevalent in the
genre. Whether you happen to be playing in a sprawl-like environment or
not, there’s exploration to be had outside of it, waiting to be discovered.
With futuristic tech, the boundaries we currently perceive, as well as the
unknown, could be altered or solved entirely.
The Wayward has a sense of the mystical or fantastical within their moves
and, like some other playbooks, could be explained with classifying it as
unknown, magical, or technological in nature. It also subverts a common
trope about cyberpunk in that there is definitely some exploration of
something more natural if the Wayward is in play. The relationship between
our world and the tech we build and discard. As well as the places in which
that waste could be found. Primarily you may find yourself trying to uncover
that dichotomy. You may have cybernetics, implying that technology may
not be bad for us necessarily. Maybe the waste is dealt with and the
impact on the environment has been lessoned, creating a more hopeful
future than cyberpunk fiction generally depicts. This touches on a new
kind of cyberpunk: solarpunk.

Inherently, the Wayward feels close to their “natural” surroundings. Where
the traditional cyberpunk media would posit humanity to have more of
an affinity to technology and shirk the natural realm—if it even still exists
in the setting—the Wayward subverts that instantly with it’s introduction.
You’re going to select what areas fond on earth you feel close to or most
interesting, creating a birthplace. You not only choose what your Homeland
is, but also what qualities it possesses and, in turn, what you look for in
others. You then tie these to a State, deciding how those particular things
make you feel; relevant when particular moves are triggered.
