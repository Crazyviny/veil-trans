THE MC MOVES
The moves an MC makes are the meat and potatoes of the mechanics. You
have your Principles and Agenda to make the world feel real. The moves
are the things that enable you to carry them out. They are the “real” tools
you have to contribute to the fiction. But what tools they are! When the
players feed you information; what they’re doing, looking for, want, think,
feel, etc. You take that information and use moves to show them the world
and the people in it. Then you in turn give them the information they need
to make their moves. In short, you reward their agency with a detailed,
interesting story and then give them their agency back.
In all likelihood, most of the time your job is going to be nice and easy.
Don’t believe me? Well...only the dice will be able to show if I’m a liar or
not, but all of this is designed to make your life easier.
If they’re getting lots of misses and weak hits, well the ball’s in your court
to showcase your cyberpunk flare, flavor, and color. And even if they’re
succeeding, so are you. Their moves are turning into more things they want
to do, win or lose—you’re just asking a lot of questions as you go while
being descriptive of the people and places they encounter, all rooted in
what they’re telling you. Having weak hits pop up a lot is the easiest way
to drive the story though, they’re still getting what they want but the moves
are also generating the cost of it. Conflict, price, hard bargains, and ugly
choices all come from their own moves.
Other times. Most times; when they’ve defused the current perceived
threat, their side of the conversation starts to putter out. Especially for
players who have played other systems and are used to a different kind
of conversation. Lots of other systems are designed for a reactive set
of circumstances to occur based on the information provided. Resulting
in less collaboration—sometimes people need to adjust to having more
agency. It’s a different kind of conversation.
When people realize their side of the conversation creates the world too,
they usually get pretty invested and that is when things get really exciting.
Anytime the conversation on that side isn’t sufficient to tell everyone what
is happening in the fiction, you’re going to tell them how the world reacts
to what they’re doing. Your contributions are different because you have
your Agenda and Principles there right in front of you, telling you what
should follow next naturally, based on what has already occurred. If you
have all these things in front of you, there is always going to be a choice
that makes sense. If you need a minute, cut to a different scene or take
a bathroom break. Just as the players need time to think, it is more than
OK when you do, too.
