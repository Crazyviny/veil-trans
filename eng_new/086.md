CYBERNETICS
Cybernetics often play a large part in cyberpunk settings and fleshing out
what cybernetics are, as well as how they are used, helps to establish
some ways in which your protagonist might interact with the world. It also
establishes some negative Tags that could come up in play that help the
MC make the technology feel real, fallible, or costly.
The Veil assumes that everyone is playing in a setting where almost
everyone has a Neurochip that grants them access to a digital
environment. They can search for information, stay in contact with others,
and perform various tasks as you or I would by using our computers. What
accessing the purely digital environment looks like is up to you and the rest
of your group. When you enter them, you present as a digital self-image
with your appearance, which is able to interact in the same manner as you
would in reality. Aside from the Neurochip, this game also assumes that
some people in the general population have cybernetics: technology that
replaces human appendages, modifying the way in which they interact
with the Neurochip, the digital environment, and other humans. People
have technological enhancements in all different ways and the Tags listed
previously are used to describe just what function these cybernetics serve
within the fiction.
You can create and have any kind of cybernetic enhancement you’d like,
assign a Tag, describing it, and as long as it is listed on your character
sheet as an option, you’re golden. If it is not, you cannot start with it.
That said, nothing is stopping you from getting modifications done postcharacter creation. All cybernetics start with a set amount of Tags that
are based on size. To begin with, the smaller the cybernetic system, the
more Tags it can have and vice versa. Because cybernetics that take up
more area could potentially be used to get more Armor or physical Tags,
they needed to be limited. Do you want to have a unique Tag with unique
functionality, or do you want enhancements that provide more protection?
If it’s small, like eyes or ears, it has three Tags. For really large ones, like
a completely cybernetic torso, one Tag. Anything in between can have
two Tags, such as an interface, something most protagonists choose to
begin play with.
For every Tag you want after that, you’ll have to handle obtaining it in
the fiction after things get rolling. There is a move, called Plasma And
Chrome detailed on page 90, designed specifically for this, which comes
into effect when you find someone that is able to operate on you in order
to enhance, remove, install, or otherwise modify any cybernetics that you
have access to.
