FICTIONAL FLAGS
Whenever a player chooses a certain playbook, pay specific attention to
why they’re choosing it and what appeals to them about it. That’s your cue,
that this is what they want to see in the game. It tells you how they interact
with the world and what types of conflicts they’re most interested in seeing.
Whenever you frame scenes, make sure you’re including those interests
and conflicts. If you don’t, chances are they’re going to wonder how they
fit into the fiction, and why they even bothered to choose that playbook.
Play to find out what happens, but think up some interesting things that
could entice that PC to get involved and interact with the fiction. Try to
give each PC an opportunity to use their playbook moves, it makes them
unique. Try and make sure that there’s something going on specifically
with every one of the people playing, as well as the overarching action
happening with everybody. If they want a character arc, help them to
make that happen.
One of the most important things to do is to consider what that playbook’s
specialty is and just give them that information. If someone is the Architect
and they’re screwing around in the digital environment, they’re going
to notice much more than other people do—in fact maybe you should
ask them what they notice. They know more about that part of The Veil
than anybody else if they have ideas about it, listen. If someone is the
Apparatus, when it comes to Artificial Intelligences and how they think
and feel and work in your world, that’s their bag. And so on with the
other playbooks.
