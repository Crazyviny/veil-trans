THE MASTER OF CEREMONIES has a lot on their shoulders in The
Veil. The players are going to answer your questions and tell you what
they’re characters are like, what they want, and how they want to advance
the fiction. They’ll tell you how they’re going to go about their own agenda.
You are going to be telling them everything else; interspersing what you
say about the world with questions about the fiction to the players.
There’s a give and take with the players and the MC and it makes for
exciting moments. The nice thing about the mechanics in this game is that
you don’t have to have some neat ideas in your head about what would
be cool before playing. What it comes down to is player’s intent, and the
dice. So really, it’s pretty low prep! Regardless of what plans you have or
what would be interesting to have happen, everybody is playing to find
out what happens next. Prepping is not worth your time, at least in large
amounts. Knowing what you want, the aesthetic, and things you want to
explore will be your prep.
Sometimes you’ll have to cross out your favorite NPC’s name that you just
introduced recently and sometimes you’ll follow the NPC’s Motivation in
the fiction, regardless of them hurting the protagonist. It’s more important
that the people in the fiction are believable, becoming more real. They are
going to do what their own agenda is, irregardless.
In the end, they’ll love that conflict just as much as you will, sometimes
not coming out on top is the most interesting thing that could have
happened. As humans, we define ourselves by the things in our lives we
have surmounted or attempted to strive for. If this never happens in the
fiction, it’s not going to feel like a believable and engaging story.
At this point, you should have read the previous chapters and made sure
you’ve understood them because you are the one people will be looking
to for guidance.
Don’t worry, it sounds hard. But when you print out your reference sheets
and look at them, in any given situation you’ll see exactly what should
happen next, I promise. There are many options and your instincts to do
what is most interesting is all it takes in so far as picking one that will
propel the story.
The next part will break down everything you need to breathe life into the
setting, as well as make believable NPCs and Threats. Likely, if you’ve
never MC’d a game before, this will be overwhelming at first, but you
will learn as you go and your growth will show within the fiction. It’s a
great feeling as well as a rewarding one, so I encourage you to stick with
it if things ever get tough. It’s important not to dwell on any perceived
mistakes and remember that at the core, you’re just playing a game
with your friends.
