ADDRESS THE PROTAGONISTS, NOT THE PLAYERS
Although it seems like a small thing, when you’re playing the game you’ll
find anything that breaks the connection with the fiction can cause some
lag in what’s going on. Bob doesn’t know what the hell is going on with his
cybernetic implant when it’s not working. He isn’t halo jumping into some
ruins from a ship with huge steampunk-like engines, is he? His protagonist
is, so talk to them! It will help them stay in character and help the fiction
stay predominant. “So, Brax, you’ve just dove off the carrier, what does
it look like up there?”
Give them details about a scene as though you were there and pointing
things out. Things that their character only sees and not what they’re
experiencing on a Meta Level. Yeah, Bob knows there’s shit down there
that he isn’t going to like. But right now Brax only sees all the gigantic
pillars jetting up into the sky as his tiny form is careening down into its
depths. If he has cyber eyes that amplify, just tell him, “Wow so you see
tiny forms down there looking up to you as you go down. It’s at 200x
amplification though, what do you do?” Don’t wait for them to think of it,
just give it to them. Then continue to describe a scene and ask what they
do. As few breaks in that layer of the game is important for immersion.
INTERWEAVE THE PROTAGONIST’S STORIES, EVEN ACROSS
DISTANCE AND BOUNDARIES
Regardless of circumstance and distance. Sometimes they will all be
doing disparate things, which is fine. Great, even. However, remember
that this is a TV show or a movie, and they are linked in some way, they
just may not know it yet (maybe you don’t either). What they’re all doing
has something to do with one another, you just need to figure out what
that is. When you get into the mindset, you’ll find the fiction will sort it
out for you. The fiction is most interesting when things are connected.
Cyberpunk is very much about problems the protagonists need to solve
that are invisible and often toying with them, on the peripheral. They’re
poking and prodding or sometimes overtly doing whatever they want and
please because they’re far too powerful to be touched at the moment. The
big overwhelming threats are going to be tackled by far more than just
one protagonist. It should be a prevailing, oppressive force on them all,
bringing them together in their struggle against it or their wish to find the
answer to something. Perhaps they’re not even aware they’re searching
for something, but the question leads them to the same place. Grinding up
against that system of control or oppression. The underdog, the unlikeable
protagonist, and the anti-hero are all hallmarks of the cyberpunk genre.
Even misfits are connected to others and events they are not aware of,
let that be the protagonist and their efforts, as well as your machinations.
