THE ATTACHED
This is could be taken very darkly, or quite innocently. Often one extreme
or the other. It could be a little friend, whose Hunger was simply driven
by wanting to be safe and close to them. It could also be a weapon that
hungers for the death of specific people, with its own agenda unraveling
as play continues.
If they pick this, they have to understand that its agenda is up to you, not
them. They should give input as to what that might be, so it is in line with
what they like and think is fun, though. However, when they’re parted they
are then themselves(as written on the playbook) implies that there is some
hold over them (known by them or not).
They probably already realize this and are relinquishing some of this
control because they want a mystery or prefer to have more prompts
than most people. Give those prompts, that mystery—whatever they seem
to be fishing for. Make the object’s desires and agenda elusive, perhaps
even elusive to you, for a time. Just make impulsive decisions about
what it wants when it enforces it’s will and the fiction will point you in the
right direction.
It also implies that they are apart from the other PCs because of their kind
of relationship between the object and themselves, appealing to players
who like to be loners. No other could understand that, so nudge another
PC or NPC to explore that aspect with them, as well as what might happen
when they start caring for people as much as the object.

 Have the object speak to them of its own will, beyond Hunger
moves, and not just when the PC talks to it.
 Ask them about their object and incorporate answers to understand
what they are hoping to get out of it and the relationship.
 Explore if the object could communicate with anyone other than the
PC, if you haven’t already done so or clarified that within the fiction.
