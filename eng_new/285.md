Look for opportunities to showcase the ideas you guys had at the first
session, springboarding off of them and incorporating new stuff. The MC
should be checking out all of the cyberpunk media they like and can think
of to get new ideas. Immerse yourself in it. Then, describe normal scenes
and put that cyberpunk twist on it. There are sky scrapers, sure. But
maybe they’re literally so tall they pierce the clouds. There’s usually a very
predominant subclass that is low-tech or using crappy equipment, maybe
even junk. There are megacorporations and there’s tech incorporated in
every area of daily routine, typically. Search out cyberpunk content on
Deviantart and Pinterest; watch movies and TV shows, read books, comics
and manga. Ghost in the Shell, Psycho-Pass, Sneakers, The Matrix, Altered
Carbon, Blade Runner, and Neuromancer—all of it. Take it all in. Decide
what you like about it and what you want to incorporate. When you ask
the players what they’re doing and what they see, ask them straight up
what about this scene looks or feels like cyberpunk. You’ll naturally have
integrated everyone’s touchstones and interests in the genre. This helps
everyone buy into the fiction and helps them feel like it’s their own story,
creating buy-in.
ALWAYS BE WORKING TOWARD THE EXPLORATION
OF THE FINAL QUESTION
When you do your first session with the players, you’ll all choose a
question you want to explore or you will. Based on the first session and
what tone you want the game to have, some people might want to be
involved in what is being explored, while others may want to just play
and learn as they go along. When you, the MC, contribute to the fiction,
always ask yourself if this is exploring or reinforcing the Final Question.
A more thorough explanation of the Final Question is found on page 361
and examples of some Final Questions can be found on page 374. For our
purposes explaining the Principle though, the nature of the collaborativebased narrative game, sometimes this won’t be possible. The end is
having the players walk away from the table asking themselves the same
question they, or you, wished to explore within the fiction. When you have
this at the forefront of your mind, along with the rest of your Principles, it
will eventually become second nature and habitual. When you work toward
this exploration of a Final Question, the fiction will feel more natural, as
there is something overarching, always. It perpetuate itself into a long term
game more easily than if there is only the direct input of the players. It is
also synonymous with cyberpunk as a genre, and science fiction. It asks
us how technology will affect us. Cyberpunk adds a few more things to the
mix, but at it’s heart is the idea that we are trying to discover or explore a
deeper question. This will be your Final Question in your fiction.
