TYPES OR DEGREES
OF MOVES
There are two moves and they differ from each other greatly and not at all.
It’s up to you how hard a move you’ll make should be. Assessing current
circumstances and what has already happened in order to make that call.
Soft moves are used to set things up for the future. They could be
opportunities the protagonists didn’t realize were there before,
foreshadowing things to come, or forecasting an immediate situation
coming to a head.
Hard moves give the players a lot less agency to act (on a sliding scale
concurrent with your fiction) because they’ve usually already had that time
and it’s passed; there are now consequences. Because of this, there’s
usually a slow progression from soft to hard moves. A soft move was used
and information was not acted upon; creating what is called a Golden
Opportunity, which then becomes a hard move. This escalation should be
in the fiction. The protagonists need to take action in order to head it off.
How clear this soft move is, ultimately, is up to you. When gauging, the
harder the move that follows, the more telegraphed it should be, though.
 SOFTEST: You hear that there’s a hit put out on your friend.
 SOFTER: The hit is being carried out as you learn of it.
 HARDER: Your friend is missing when you get there.
 HARDEST: When you arrive there, it’s already too late.
Using your Principles as guidelines, decide how hard a move should be.
Always do what would be most interesting while also in line with best
practices. Keeping them in constant threat without any interpersonal play
is usually going to feel like the fiction is about one thing: action.
Not many stories have tons of danger and no time to learn about the
protagonists. You’re not their fan if you keep trying to kill them over and
over and over, either. Conversely, you’re not making things costly if they’re
simply handed opportunities. Sure, sometimes things should be easy,
or feel easy. The price should be made apparent later, after they have
enjoyed that reprieve.
If you need an easy way to think of a soft move versus hard, distill it to
the amount of agency the protagonist has to act from the information
they’re getting from you. If they are able to change what the outcome of
the event is, it’s soft. If it’s done with, happening right now, or already
happened and they’re going to have to react to the change that’s already
occurred, it’s hard.
