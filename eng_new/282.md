ALWAYS SAY…
The Veil demands that the MC adhere to the Agenda, but also the
Principles. When there’s a lot going on and multiple agendas are brought
to a head, or you get too attached to a Threat or NPC, always say:
 …what the Principles demand.
 …what the rules demand.
 …what your prep work demands.
 …what honesty demands.
The Agenda and the rules are the bones of the game. If you break them,
the fiction and the game as a whole will not function properly any longer.
If a protagonist does something that should cause them Harm, inflict it.
If a roll you were rooting for comes up a miss, accept it and make a move
that follows the fiction. Maybe the dice come up a hit when a protagonist
is up against an NPC that you put your heart into, maybe even one that
was trying to help them, they just never found out. Either way the rules
must never change. Discipline must always come first in The Veil, or the
fiction will ultimately fall apart.
The Veil balances everything by having you actively engage with your
Principles and Agenda before making a move. If an NPC would seek
revenge, they do so. If they can extort a protagonist towards their own
ends, they do so. Wash your hands of it. It’s entirely in the hands of the
fiction and the dice. Just as the protagonists have a huge impact on the
game, so do the NPCs. The players may not see it, but your Agenda and
Principles are engraved on every person out there. They’re in every piece of
tech, every neon sign, and every mag pistol being aimed. And that is where
the balance is, all of the unforeseen haphazard play comes together in a
cohesive story that surprises everyone this way. And while this occurring,
we get to explore something more because you’re also always embedding
a question in the fiction that is being explored by you and the players both
(more on that on Page 361).
That being said, the players only know what you tell them during the
conversation. Assume they won’t extrapolate something or take a
hint. They won’t know you’re being obtuse in order to get them to
explore a situation or person. They only know what you tell them, you
must be honest and liberal with information. Don’t hide things from
them. Especially if it’s something they would have described their
