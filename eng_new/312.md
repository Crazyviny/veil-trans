DEFINING THE VEIL
The Veil is like our Internet now, except that everybody has a Neurochip in
their heads and is constantly connected to this wealth of information while
also being connected to each other. The Veil is also a nebulous presence
within that Internet which has a will of its own. As some playbooks suggest,
you can enter The Veil, this purely digital space, with your mind. Other than
these concepts though, it is largely nebulous and undefined—by design.
Because it is a digital environment, people should be able to bend and
even break aspects of that world; like in The Matrix. It might also be
possible for people to have a level of control over themselves that is even
greater than in reality, if they are even aware that they are in it at all, at
that specific time.
One of the ways you might define it is by the success and failures of the
moves being triggered. When the players know they are in the digital
environment, attempt something that seems uncanny or defies reality in
some way, and they roll a miss—ask each other if it was a miss because
that was just not a possible thing to do. Creating the “laws” of the realm
as you go, using the dice as a tool. From the discussion of whether or not
it is possible you’ll learn what want and expect from this digital realm,
and subvert or use those expectations to your advantage. What The Veil
is, what it wants, what the purpose of it is, will change with each game.
Pay close attention to Lift The Veil. When it says that the MC will ask
them a few questions and they must answer them, that is a prompt for
you, MC. You should always be asking them things and when you do,
this information should always be incorporated. It may treat each PC
completely differently, subjective to their relationship to it. It might display
or give access to something or someone that it would not for another
character. When they interact with The Veil, they want something from
it. During that exchange, The Veil is posing questions to them in turn,
which should reveal to you its Motivation during play, bit by bit. The digital
environment is an aspect of The Veil, it is also a vehicle for its will. It’s
another tool to get to know more about the protagonists and create very
personal and tailored events and scenes just for them.
The first questions posed to the player from Lifting The Veil should be
about their own view, how they access The Veil and what that means to
the character in the fiction. Then start getting more personal and even
down right strange. What’s your favorite color? Whom do you most miss?
Then incorporate it. When you’re describing scenes after that, use those
colors. If they have a secret, make The Veil try to guess at that secret. If
they hide their true identity from everybody else, make street signs and
coffee shops have names guessing at their identity wherever they go.
When they walk past skyscrapers the person they miss the most is there
in the glass for a second and then not when they look again.
