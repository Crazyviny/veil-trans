WORLD BUILDING
At this point, turn to the MC and do your first session stuff. There is a
setting playbook included that can be used as an optional world building
tool. There are questions designed to populate your fiction with things you
perhaps hadn’t thought of before. You don’t need to answer all of them
right off the bat either—sometimes the best thing to do is just start playing
and fill in these questions as you go. Make sure you and everyone else
uses the Link move to establish at least one NPC you have in your life and
your relationship to them, if your fiction calls for it. If the fiction feels sparse
or people are stuck, the Link move is a great start to kick off the fiction.
When you mark down Obligation on one another, it’s helpful to make a
relationship map or write a short note regarding the Obligation, as they
should be referenced when used.
It can really add another dimension of play if you all bring with you
cultural or societal aspects you’d like to explore in the fiction, as well as
technological ones. What are some interesting cultural things that differ
from today, or are similar that you’d like to breach, when compared to
the culture you know? How will technologies of today change society and
the aspects of everyday life? As you play you can fill these out, bringing
some in that you thought of previously from touchstones in the genre.
Perhaps you’ve thought of some cultural examples when creating your
protagonist as well.
The fiction will become more engaging the more you contribute to it.
Your first session of play usually is just a free play experience with little to
nothing pre-planned. The intention is to springboard off what you’ve just
talked about wanting to see in the fiction. After which, the MC is going to
take what you started within that first session and mold it into something
that incorporates all the things you liked about it and, using their tools, a
campaign for everyone to play in. Cutting out all the stuff you didn’t like
about the first session experience and preserving only what was fun for
everyone. If some things feel off about your character, or you don’t like
specific things, change them by all means. The first session is precisely
for finding out what you like, what you don’t like, and tweaking everything
to become precisely the story you want to participate in.
The most important thing to remember about the fiction is to be an active
member of the narrative—to the point that makes you comfortable only.
The more active you are in your scenes, the more you will most likely be
able to get out of them. Everyone at the table is responsible for making
everyone else feel included, spotlighted, and safe—not just the MC. Don’t
be afraid to invite other players to be participants in your own scenes
and always skip the minutia when it gets boring! Ask to frame a scene
that tests your Belief. Be a fan of your fiction and the characters within it.
