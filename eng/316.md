PC-NPC-TRIANGLES
It’s true that NPCs aren’t complex with their Motivations and that is
the guiding question when wielding an NPC. When they do pursue their
Motivation, it doesn’t mean that’s the only side of them ever presented
on screen. What one PC experienced from them could be vastly different
based on PC choices. If they need information and try to manipulate with
logic or a deal, versus threatening them and making them look weak
around other people, the NPC logically will react very differently. The way
that NPC acts towards each PC could be, and should be, quite different,
based on these events.
Some NPCs may be stupid; some extremely smart and manipulative.
When Rags went after Cricket in the first place, he went into an NPC’s
establishment with a show of force and threatened Cricket’s friend,
Dremmer. Also an NPC, Dremmer felt threatened and told Rags something
he thought he’d want to know in order to gain his trust and to get him to
leave and stop threatening him. Dremmer’s Motivation is to eventually
take down Rags and restore order to this place, but he’d never be able to
do that if Rags had discovered this information. He instead threw a friend
under the bus and succeeded in getting rid of Rags and being able to
continue in his Motivation of taking him down; eventually.

NPC OBLIGATION
Sometimes, NPCs will want an Obligation from PCs. More often than not,
it’s the opposite. Obligation is person-specific so the Obligation needs to be
tracked from one NPC to the next. Not every NPC is controlled by the MC.
Flip the setting playbook around and make an Obligation relationship map
for this in order to make sure it is visible and in everyone’s thoughts when
moving from one session to the next. Some examples of how Obligation
could be accrued by an NPC interacting with a PC are:
 When a PC offers them Obligation in exchange for something they
want and they accept.
 When an NPC does them a favor without being given Cred.
 When a PC rolls a 7–9 when they roll to Refute Obligation, and the
Obligation owed increases.
Whenever they have Obligation on a PC they can use the Obligation moves,
just as PC’s do to collect on the Obligation. Whenever an NPC does collect
on the Obligation, make sure it feels right in the fiction. If they go to a
Seeker to do something that has nothing to do with them or something
they’re not good at, you may not be a fan of the player. Collecting on
Obligation is a great way to begin a new thread to pull on for the PCs and
further the Motivation of the NPC or Threat in play.
