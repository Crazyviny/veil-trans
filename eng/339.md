you frame a scene with just one protagonist from the get-go, they
might think that that’s what this game is going to be like the entire
time without discussing it. They may never say anything despite
not having a good time because they expected to be in more scenes
with people. This game supports that mode of play a lot. But for the
first session, have them explore if that should be the case or not before
the idea gets cemented into your fiction.
Mix it up, let them get into trouble from the get go, to some degree. Show
them how moves work when in a conflict and give them a situation to
showcase how moves snowball naturally and what each basic move is
for. Get them into a confrontation and ask them why it’s happening.
Have some aggressive NPCs and see how they react. Get a sense of
what kind of protagonist they will be. Sometimes people think they want
to be super badass and break teeth every chance they get, but then when
it comes down to it they act differently than they expected, maybe even
surprising themselves. In the first session and after it, if they want to
change anything about themselves, let them do it. Otherwise they might
feel regretful that they’ve picked the playbook at all. Finding this stuff
out and tweaking the fiction to everyone’s tastes is how you’ll get a lively,
engaged table for the rest of your play together.
