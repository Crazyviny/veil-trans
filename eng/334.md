ESTABLISHING
EXPECTATIONS
When you’re exploring mature questions about the future, it’s ripe for
conflict between players. One of the most important conversations you’ll
have is the one you have before you start playing. Set the expectations
for what people want to see in the game and what their comfort level
is. With a class like the Seeker, which has Faith-related situations and
questions associated with it, sometimes people can get uncomfortable.
That’s important to take into consideration. It sucks when people don’t
get what they want out of the game and when people just don’t have a
good time, in general! Ask them what they liked and disliked each session
and tailor it around everyone’s enjoyment. Don’t forget to count yourself
among those people!.
Please use the X card, especially if you don’t know each other that well.
The Veil can be unforgiving and the system can seem punishing to some
players. You can do horrible things to others and witness terrible things
yourselves. It makes for some great drama and interplay, but it also can
make some problems. Have the expectations conversation. Every. Time.
 What is this game going to be rated? R? PG?
 Is there going to be sex, how detailed will we get with it if so? What
constitutes an intimate moment?
 Is this going to be focused on emotions, interpersonal play, or lighthearted fun with lots of action?
 Sex, drugs, crime? What’s off the table if people already know what
makes them uncomfortable?
 Are topics like race, gender, sexual identity, or class going to be
OK topics to touch on? How about religion, politics etc? Check out
the cultural and technological aspects people specifically want to
touch on in the setting playbook.
 Are you going to keep within the tropes of cyberpunk or subvert
them? Are you going to be the extroverted cop with cybernetics,
who’s well-adjusted and wants to solve the problem (subverting
some tropes), rather than an introvert with poor social skills who’s
often understood (fairly typical cyberpunk protagonist)? What
appeals to you and the other players, why?
 Will you defy stereotypes? Is this future the same as today,
reflecting current society? How will the world have changed?
 Are you in the same geographical location as where you are now?
Do you know enough about the culture you’re planning on having
your protagonists to play to not default to biases about it?
