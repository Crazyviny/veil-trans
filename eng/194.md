There are a few areas on the second page of the Executive playbook that
need to be read over carefully, then filled in. These areas are:
 The area titled “The Board.”
Whatever establishment you decide the megacorp to be, you answer to
these particular individuals. What intentions they have for this corporate
entity you serve will be expressed by way of the contracts you will be
tasked with each session; pick ones you think would be the most
interesting to interact with in t he fiction. That way, no matter what you’re
tasked with you’re still having a fun time portraying your protagonist as you
struggle to divine their goals, intentions, and most likely, hidden agenda
beyond their actions.
REX: I think the board at Solaris Tech Inc. Or STI, are pretty

big dirt bags. They want to get ahead no matter the cost
so I’m going to choose, “Anytime, anything is lucrative,
no matter the affiliations, they take it.” Let’s see…I think
I like the image of these guys shrouded in mystery like
the ever-mysterious smoking man. Which brings me to
a solid +1 Capital.
 The area titled “Contracts.”
When you’re not balancing your work given to you by the board, you can
also do your own contracts and reap the benefits when they’re fulfilled.
Assuming they do get finished, that is. If you can do both you stand to gain
a lot but possibly over extend yourself as well. Choose wisely!
 The area titled “Power.”
This section is purely to get you thinking about how you obtained this
power. Did you claw your way up from lower status, was it always going
to be this way, or did something else entirely happen to land you in your
current position?
REX: I think Farooq totally rose up from a lowborn status or

something similar. Maybe conned his way up and he’s
a rising star, looking to run the show one day.
 The area titled “Mandatory Contracts.”
These contracts are not like your other paying contracts. These are specific
things the board tasks you to do. Though each contract does not have to
be a completely task, they could be, depending on what the MC thinks is
appropriate. For instance, three contracts could be pulling off one specific
task or job, or three different ones entirely. They are accompanied by
a timeline, always, as well as the resources up front to get them done.
Though you can choose to hire people to see to these tasks without
you, the consequences of not fulfilling them can be dire and snowball,
hamstringing some of your other moves. It is wise to make sure they
always get done—from a mechanical standpoint.
