PICKING AN ADVANCEMENT
On every character sheet there is a list of improvements available for them
to take. Most are self-explanatory like +1 Mad, +1 Sad, +1 Scared etc.
Increase your State by +1 and when you roll you’ll be a bit better when you
take action in the fiction with t hat State. What that increase looks like in
the fiction could be touched on as an interesting bit of characterization
for your protagonist, too. There are options for you to pick new moves on
your playbook, as well as options to take moves from another playbook.
When this happens, it does not mean you get their options and extras that
playbooks ordinarily begin play with. You only get that specific move from
it. Work with the MC to provide within the fiction, if applicable, why and
what that might look like. If you choose to erase an Obligation, it should
still be explained in fiction so the story makes sense and everyone is on
the same page.
When you shift from one playbook to another entirely, you are the same
protagonist but modified. You get the starting moves from that playbook
and can work with the MC as to what would be appropriate to migrate with
you (if anything) to that new playbook. It’s possible that old moves would
move with you—if it makes sense in the fiction. Sometimes it really doesn’t
make sense that it would, work together to figure it out. The purpose
of changing playbooks is not to increase the mechanical benefits you
get from the system, but to get the most out of that character for story
purposes. Or, it could be to explore another aspect of your character and
this was the best method to achieve it. It may not even make sense for
you to carry over your State modifiers as they are listed now on the current
playbook, with such a fundamental change happening—perhaps they
aren’t the same emotionally, either. Something to consider.
Some playbooks will naturally lead from one to or digress into one another.
As you play certain playbooks and look into others, you may see what your
natural progression will be for that particular protagonist and what might
be the next step logical step for them in the fiction, then work toward it.
It’s important to note that not every playbook has the same advancement
selections. Make sure you take a look at each specific section to
familiarize yourself with how that playbook specifically handles their
own improvement.
