You can also use moves to help frame scenes, If you want. Create a
move in order to skip looking for information and just have it based on
a roll. Maybe they want to abstract it, have the roll, and see if there are
complications executing it. If there are, that’s the next scene you frame
with them. A group is going to have its own things it finds interesting, so
try and zoom in on those as much as possible. If people want to abstract
something but it’s also a situation where you feel it could turn out one
way or another, chances are a custom move is the nice middle ground
you’re looking for.
If players miss a session and need to be caught up, you can create custom
moves to solve that as well. Alternatively if you have an interaction in mind
to set off a one-shot game you could create a custom move to dictate the
opening circumstances or establish backstory.
In the original Apocalypse World, which this game relies on for mechanics
and framework, the “Advanced Fuckery” chapter included is the original
framework explained for Powered by the Apocalypse games. If there are
more things you want to do and check out, I advise looking at that chapter
in particular. The writer goes in-depth about the system, custom moves,
and a lot of other ideas, even efforts from other players.
