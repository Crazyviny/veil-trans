THREATS
Prep work is not the same as plotting out the future. It’s solidifying what
has already occurred in the fiction and committing to the Motivation of an
NPC that will end up changing the fiction substantially. When you want to
inject a plot, what you actually want in The Veil is a Threat.
Threats are a narrative force of nature. They change what’s around them
with their wants and needs and they’re the big players in your world.
Eventually, you’ll also need to create a Main Antagonist, which is a special
Threat that exerts itself on the status quo of the established fiction far
more than any normal Threat. It is something that unifies and focuses
the Threats, with or without their knowing—it upends the current known
fiction by directly asserting itself, primarily in order to explore the Final
Question. It is also what will mainly drive the future of your world, aside
from the protagonists themselves.
The prep work you do becomes a part of The Veil organically, the same as
when the fiction changes from the protagonists taking action, or not doing
so in the fiction. Solidifying a Threat occurs when you take your thoughts
about a Threat and put it on paper, for good. When you do this, you are
saying that these Threats are set within the fiction now. For better or worse,
they are now defined and pursue that concrete Motivation you’ve given
them. They are as defined as they will get because you know what they
will pursue and how they intend to do so. Everything else is the work of
the fiction itself, not you.
These decisions about your Threats matter as much as any moves made
by the protagonists and the ramifications of them; truly.
The reason why you take the time between each session to do prep work,
is to make sure your idea of these Threats is cemented in your mind. We
translate that idea about what the Threat is into a type of Threat.
If you change a Threat’s type, it should be because the fiction dictates it.
Otherwise you’re uprooting the established fiction. How the Threat acts
needs to be in line with what’s happened so far. If the type does not
make sense it should be changed. If you thought they were one type but
are another, do so. Keep in mind though, that changing a Threat type to
something that does not reflect the fiction will make the Threat feel out
of place, resulting in bad fiction—most likely.
The only thing that should ever change a Threat type once committed is
whatever happens during the upcoming session.
