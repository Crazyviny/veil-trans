 The area titled “Obligation.”
The Honorbound is rooted in Obligation, the concept of which is to owe
someone else, not with money, but a social currency. You feel like you
should make things right or there’s a mutual understanding that you
owe them and should repay them. This is detailed more in depth in the
Obligation section of the book along with the moves pertaining to it. Know
that as the Honorbound, you’re defining Obligation completely, other than
of course, the standard Obligation moves referenced in that section.
You operate out of an institution or organization that people come to
when people don’t adhere to their Obligation and want recompense. Your
selections to describe this place, who you know there, what it looks like,
and some allies within it—who have come there over time—are not as
important as the options which define Obligation itself in your setting.
Most important is choosing 3–5 circumstances that incur Obligation. By
choosing these they become a part of your setting permanently, as well as
the punishment for not adhering to the Obligation that is then owed. For
example: if you select that employing someone incurs Obligation, everyone
in society now knows that employment comes with it a debt of some kind.
If the employer wants something from them, collecting on said debt, and
they cannot or will not, what is the punishment for that?
These are some of the decisions you will be making and, as the enforcer
for this currency, you will also be the one sent out to directly deal with
these types of situations in order to rectify the problem. You personally
may be carrying out the chosen punishment. Make sure you’re picking
options you find compelling and want to see in the fiction. Also, be
absolutely positive these punishments are things you want to be doing
and visualizing within in the fiction.
As you’re making your way through the selections, chat with the
other players and bounce ideas off of them. If anything makes them
uncomfortable, see if there’s something as interesting that you could
substitute for it.
