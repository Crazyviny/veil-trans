Each playbook is unique. Only one playbook may be in play at any given
time at the table. Decide what most appeals to you, then take the playbook
you want and read it over. Please note that I have included examples
where I thought necessary only for the purposes of clarification; the
examples are not meant to shoehorn in a specific interpretation of the
playbook or how they’re meant to be played, that’s left to you and your
vision for your protagonist.
**Choosing playbooks is important because each one is like a specific
ingredient in your setting. Each brings implications and each makes it
so other playbooks may not make sense in the setting when paired with
it. The playbooks are inspired by a wide range of fiction in order to give
you ample choice and opportunity to assemble a kind of fiction you want.
Each selection modifies something, and so you should be open at the
table and talk about your choices, as they impact the tone and kind of
story that will happen. A story without an Onomastic being hunted by the
Iconoclasts, for instance, is very different than one without. The characters
are non-traditional compared to some roleplaying games, in that they
contribute a lot of world building simply from you making the choices
necessary on the playbook.
As people are choosing you lose sight of what the setting might look like,
always clarify what you are thinking and rif off of one another, in order to
satisfy all parties involved at the table**.
